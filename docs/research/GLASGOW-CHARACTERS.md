### Visual brief for the original encounter

- **Adult human drummer:** seated naturally, with supported feet and bent knees, holding two wooden sticks. Animate the arms so grips remain plausible and tips strike the intended bin surfaces; no standing tinkerer/spanner pose.
- **Container kit:** a compact arc of recognizable cylindrical bin/tub props, with rolled rims/ribbed sides and simple stands/supports. Include readable circular striking surfaces and variation in container size. A low front/side container or one cymbal can explain the setup without reproducing the exact photographed layout.
- **Two soft toy cats:** two small original fabric/plush props at the front on a low street mat/base, with stuffed rounded bodies, soft ears/paws, seam/material cues and gentle bob/sway/paw movement. Two is the authorized adaptation, not a claim about the number/species in every source. Avoid exposed motor shells, piston legs, metal eyes and a workshop turntable.
- **Street-performance context:** compact pavement-scale arrangement, original simple donation/performance cues and original local percussion. The 2016 human and 2019 mascot variants inform different observed features; the game does not need the giant costume. Preserve the actual A/B/C skating approaches and earned/queued payoff rules.
- **Evidence gate:** inspect actual medium, approach, earned payoff and reduced-motion frames, plus bounded motion, for seated anatomy, grips/stick-tip contacts, recognizable bins, both cloth cats and clear skating space. ## Character rules derived for this game

Make the local character a participant with a point of view: a busker wants a strong finale; a market trader wants an absurd delivery kept intact; a park regular sets an improbably precise line. Use warm understatement and a small amount of understandable local phrasing. Caption every instruction and keep the objective readable without understanding dialect.

Use **fictional people**, original clothing and dialogue. “Mo” is an original musician, not a renamed depiction of one of the sources. The cleared generic MakeHuman anatomical derivative supplies a human head/hands; original garments and instrument supply the rest. There is no claim of a Glasgow accent: the encounter has captions and animation, with no new voice recording. Its quiet spatial instrument is original local PCM synthesis of an open chord, with no imported song or source performance.

The owner's river-prank suggestion is captured as a design direction for harmless, fictional slapstick. Make floating foam props, an inflatable mascot or an explicitly willing festival stunt crew the target. Avoid making a real local performer or a caricature of poverty/addiction the butt of a violent challenge. This preserves the cheeky physical gag and gives it a visible float/rescue payoff.
