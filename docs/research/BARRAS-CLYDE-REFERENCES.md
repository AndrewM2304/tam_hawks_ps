# Barrowland, Barras and central Clyde: inspected references

**Inspected:** 17 October 2026. **Scope:** visual reference for the original, compressed game district; continuation of M03-006/M03-007 and the [Glasgow dossier](GLASGOW_REFERENCES.md). This is evidence for identifiable architecture, not a measured map, current business directory or art acceptance result. Implementation decisions belong in [the level brief](../levels/BARRAS-CLYDE-BRIEF.md).

Actual photographs and all four pages of the official market plan were downloaded and visually opened. Official market, Glasgow City Heritage Trust (GCHT), Historic Environment Scotland (HES) and Wikimedia pages were read. No paid API was used. All downloaded photographs/PDFs remain in ignored `.local/barras-clyde-research/`; none is a game texture, logo asset or imported layout. Production uses original geometry/materials and an original interpretation of the venue lettering.

## Barrowland frontage: what is visible

**Spelling and hierarchy:** B1/B2 show singular, mixed-case **Barrowland**, with a large initial B and lower-case following letters. The word slants right, using rounded outlined strokes. The B has a prominent looping/descending left swash; the tall l/d and long word silhouette matter at chase-camera distance. No exact commercial font has been identified. A generic upright uppercase `BARROWLANDS` plaque loses these observed features.

**Stars and light:** several large five-point outline stars sit above and below the word; smaller coloured stars scatter around the perimeter. Fine rays fan from the word toward the larger stars. The night exposure shows bright warm yellow/white inner strokes, red intermediate strokes and a turquoise/green outer edge. GCHT separately describes red, yellow, green and blue neon. Exposure and animation phase change the appearance: use this colour hierarchy without claiming an exact calibrated palette, star count or flashing sequence. Daylight B2 makes the unlit star outlines and rays easier to distinguish.

**Building, not just sign:** the lettering spans a broad, shallow-looking upper facade with a regular dark mullion/panel grid in grey/green tones. The sign occupies much of that elevation. A low row of market/shop entrances and shutters sits below; a narrow pale entrance strip appears at the right. B2 shows pebbledash-like street piers and a narrow lower window strip. The inspected frontage does not support a small grey sign on an otherwise generic tall red-brick block. B7 page 2 independently diagrams the sign above several market entrance bays in section A.

**Game interpretation:** retain the observed word silhouette, star/ray hierarchy, broad panelled frontage and market-bay base. Build original paths/meshes for lettering and decoration. Any reduction in bay count, star arrangement, total width or entrance placement is a legibility/performance choice, not a measured reconstruction. Do not copy a downloaded sign image onto a rectangle.

## Barras market: circulation and material cues

B3 shows continuous streets between long low market halls and taller brick/stone neighbours. Green `McIVERS MARKETS` fascias, repeated open bays, flagpoles, projecting awnings and tarpaulin roofs form the edges. Red iron `THE BARRAS` gateways mark entries across the lane. Clothes racks, tables, produce, cards and rolled goods occupy the margins. One image visibly identifies **Suffolk St.** at opposing corners. These photos establish historical character; they do not establish today's traders or exact clear widths.

Four images presently served by the official B6 site were also inspected:

- [Exterior lane](https://barrowland.co.uk/cdn/shop/files/market_tab_1024x.webp?v=1730402180): green corrugated hall wall, repeated open doors, red flagpole brackets, pavement racks and tables, a taller red-stone neighbour and a gateway beyond.
- [Decorative goods stall](https://barrowland.co.uk/cdn/shop/files/b_c5c94a2a-d970-4381-b5d7-c08c9e0109bf_1024x.jpg?v=1730402480): densely hung lampshades, textiles and furniture under a practical covered roof.
- [Small-goods stall](https://barrowland.co.uk/cdn/shop/files/d_1024x.jpg?v=1725889935): tabletop merchandise and hanging packaged goods fill a compact bay.
- [Indoor fashion row](https://barrowland.co.uk/cdn/shop/files/a_1024x.jpg?v=1725889910): green stall uprights, orange/brown open triangular roof trusses, translucent roof strips and suspended linear lights above clothes rails; a handmade row sign hangs overhead.

Their capture dates are unknown. URL cache parameters are not photograph dates. The images corroborate mixed old/new trading and dense, varied stock; do not reproduce pictured products, people or stall artwork as textures.

B6's operator description places the market between Gallowgate and London Road, extending from Bain Street toward Glasgow Cross. It identifies indoor/outdoor trading and distinct entrances. B7 page 1 shows A–D hall groups, open ground and several gateways; page 2 shows market entrances under the Barrowland frontage; page 3 shows B/C rows and cross-aisles labelled Sustainable Fashion Row and Hippie Lane; page 4 shows section D rows plus outdoor trading ground. It does **not** provide reliable dimensions or a street-labelled plan from which to derive game coordinates.

**Game interpretation:** make an understandable passage through opposing hall/stall edges, interrupted by cross-aisles and a second way out. Concentrate goods, corrugation, roof trusses and small colour variation at the margins, keeping an explicit skateable line and readable landing space. A market challenge can use entry, aisle and exit markers or existing trick opportunities; its invented route/score conditions are gameplay design. The agreed game aisle X110–118, Z18 to −5 and shop edges X108/120 are authored coordinates, not source measurements. Existing broad-court and hall placements may be compressed or relocated transparently.

No specific Barras mural was sufficiently verified in this pass to justify reproducing it. Original mural-like decoration can be labelled fictional; the hall/gateway/stock/roof structure has stronger inspected evidence.

## Central Clyde: bridge, quay and opposite bank

**South Portland Street suspension bridge:** C1 describes a 414 ft single span, approximately 126 m, with classical triumphal-arch pylons in polished honey-coloured sandstone. Fluted Ionic columns, Doric pilasters, a deep plain frieze/cornice and chains passing through the frieze define the portal. The deck uses lattice girders and flat-link suspension chains; the walkway is tarmac, with thin lattice parapets and lamp brackets. These are official architectural details, not inferred from a thumbnail.

C2/C3 visibly corroborate two stone arch portals, sagging red chain bundles, slender red vertical hangers, dark frames with pale diagonal diamond-lattice railings and traditional black lamp fittings. The deck is relatively slender. C3 looks south through the portal toward a long joined terrace; grass, railings and trees mediate between terrace and river. For a recognisable original silhouette, prioritise portals, chain curve, lattice and lamps over extra generic bridge piers.

**Carlton Place:** C4 describes Peter Nicholson's 1802–04 classical three-storey-and-basement, 35-bay pedimented terrace. It has an ashlar front, channelled ground floor, a central Doric portico, ordered upper windows and cast-iron balconies. C2/C3 show a long pale joined river-facing frontage, rather than isolated identical shop blocks. An original shortened terrace may use that continuous cornice/window rhythm, occasional pediment and darker slate roof without claiming all 35 bays or exact present condition.

**Quay and urban depth:** C5 shows broad reflective brown/green water, a low sequence of stone arches at Glasgow/Jamaica Bridge and a separate red/white lattice railway structure with overhead gantries behind it. At the visible bank, dark water-stained block retaining walls meet the water; an upper promenade/handrail sits above a lower ledge and vegetation. Trees, varied roof heights and a distant dome give depth. C2 also shows bank masonry, vegetation and ample open water. This supports layered bank/river/bridge/skyline relationships, not a narrow drainage trench with disconnected inland fronts.

**Game interpretation:** preserve a broad water plane, visibly separate quay and upper promenade levels, a continuous opposite-bank edge, and a distinctive bridge silhouette. The existing 38 m game channel and bridge near X−98 are intentional compression; a South Portland-derived visual treatment there is a composite, not a claim that its real span/location is 38 m. Keep accepted collision, recovery and crossing routes explicit. Mooring fittings, ships, cranes, Custom House/Clutha facades and specific industrial silhouettes were not sufficiently verified here; none is required to communicate the inspected central-Clyde character.
