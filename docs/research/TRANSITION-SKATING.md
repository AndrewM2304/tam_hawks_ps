## Recommendation

Keep Godot 4.6.3, `CharacterBody3D` and the existing street controller. For the isolated experiment, use an **explicit surface-contact state with a full 3D tangent and support normal**, following GodotSkate's pipe-state/contact-frame approach. Selectively adapt Not Pro Skater's support-continuity and edge-release guards. Both are MIT code references, but neither controller is a drop-in replacement or verified on our engine/physics settings.

GodotSkate makes the body’s `up_direction` follow confirmed support and aligns the body to that frame. That is the more direct candidate for contact through 90°, whereas our yaw-only travel direction degenerates when projected onto a vertical face. Not Pro Skater offers especially useful distinctions between a curved surface continuing under the board and a genuine lip/drop, plus a separately modelled assisted vert air. Its global 80° floor setting and position snaps should not be copied wholesale into the current city.

Start with one low quarter-pipe and its supported bank bypass. Prove its contact, rollback, release and re-entry before considering a bowl. A quarter-pipe result does not establish closed-bowl carving, coping stalls, lip tricks, spine transfers or wallrides. Leave those variants explicit and open.

- Lines 309–354 own air-entry eligibility resets and a single true landing, including spin resolution and revert eligibility. A support-frame update cannot call `_begin_air()`/`_finish_landing()` each time a curved mesh changes triangle; that would manufacture trick opportunities and landings.

## Proposed bounded fixture and state rules

These are proposed experiment values and contracts, not installed behavior or accepted tuning.

**Original fixture:** an 8 m-wide cylindrical quarter-pipe, radius 2.4 m, a flat 18 m approach, a 3 m rear deck, and a separate 4 m-wide side bank/bypass at no more than 25°. Use shared sampled vertices for the visible surface and static collision. At a toe origin, an authored cross-section is `(x, R*(1-cos(theta)), -R*sin(theta))`, with `x` within ±4 m. Sample at no more than 2° increments; cap the back/sides and keep the playable face correctly wound. Start at 80° (rise ≈1.98 m), then test a separate 90° / 2.4 m-rise variant with a short vertical segment. Passing 80° does not pass the 90° case. No prototype geometry enters a career route.

The bank bypass joins both the lower approach and deck, with honest support/collision throughout. Remove it temporarily only in a negative diagnostic, never from the candidate presented as safely playable. A later curved bowl pocket requires a separate azimuth-changing surface test; this first straight quarter-pipe cannot prove that generalization.

| Phase | Admission and movement | Exit / score boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary approach | Existing controller until a real probe/sweep confirms a specifically tagged transition face within a bounded contact distance. Tags provide eligibility, not invisible support. | Enter support state without an air/landing/trick event. An ordinary wall or untagged bank must retain ordinary behavior. |
| Supported transition | Store world contact point, normal, full tangent heading/velocity and surface identity. Turn within that tangent plane; integrate projected gravity and explicit brake/drag. Preserve signed movement through zero so insufficient speed rolls back. The first no-push diagnostic must not add pump energy. | Ordinary floor exit restores world-up frame and heading aligned to actual outgoing horizontal travel, preserving speed. Contact loss/side edge/intentional ollie releases once. A normal-frame update alone is not a new air segment. |
| Vert air | Enter only from a confirmed steep eligible face, with actual upward departure and finite outgoing velocity. Free gravity is the baseline. If a return assist is needed, evaluate a separate explicitly logged variant using the retained takeoff plane/normal, bounded velocity correction and a non-scoring return turn. | No position teleport, unbounded attraction, or gravity reduction hidden as momentum preservation. No same-frame reattachment after an intentional ollie. Missing support or leaving the finite feature must yield ordinary air/recovery, not an infinite invisible plane. |
| Re-entry | A descending real shape/contact probe must reach the front of the eligible face with valid clearance. Confirm supported travel before adopting the new normal; reject backface/underside/obstacle hits. | Resolve existing aerial awards and emit one real landing, then open the existing revert/bank window. Supported transition travel is not perpetual combo continuation. Repeated triangle contacts cannot refresh the window. |
| Rollback / safe exit | Tangent gravity returns a slow rider toward the toe without an automatic ollie. Keep sign and contact normal finite around zero speed; avoid a camera flip when horizontal speed briefly vanishes. | Rejoin ordinary ground without a fabricated landing/award. Manual entry on the steep prototype face is disabled; any existing manual ends once at entry under the documented link-end rule. |
| Bail / reset | Obstruction, invalid frame or failed recovery resolves through the existing bail/reset path. Do not alter ordinary heading-only forgiving landings globally. | Clear feature identity, collision orientation, support grace, air assist and camera state. Preserve already banked score; discard only the active combo. Pause and restart behave exactly as elsewhere. |

For the prototype contact frame, GodotSkate's normal-relative `up_direction` and frame alignment are candidates; the whole root need not rotate if an isolated adapter consistently transforms its collider/visuals. That alternative is an engineering decision to test, not an established implementation. Initial substeps should limit travel to 0.10 m and normal-frame change to 5°, with overlap checks at candidate orientations as well as translated capsule sweeps. Godot's translated `test_move` is not proof of a continuous rotational sweep. If the oriented capsule cannot remain clear and visually aligned, reject the frame strategy before integration rather than hide clipping with a smaller invisible collider.

A first support candidate may use a ≤0.10 m normal correction and a brief continuity allowance, always bounded by actual nearby eligible collision. Exact thresholds must be recorded with traces. Reject a convex lip, removed collider, outward departure or excessive correction; no grace period may keep a board skating on empty air. Keep ordinary 56° street settings unchanged outside the experiment.

**Camera proposal:** retain a world-up horizon and decouple chase yaw from the steep support normal. Keep the last useful horizontal heading near a vertical apex/rollback; blend toward the outgoing direction after it becomes well-defined. Preserve reduced-motion settings. Check the actual camera path and rider visibility through contact/air/re-entry; the current ray-corrected desired point does not prove that the smoothed path or near plane stays outside a wall. Introduce no forced cinematic takeover.

## Required evidence before promotion

Use real input/physics progression after fixture setup. Initial placement or declared entry-speed fixtures are acceptable when labelled; no per-frame teleporting or direct support-state assignment may serve as evidence that entry works. Log each physics sample: phase, surface ID, position, contact point/normal/distance, requested and post-slide velocity, actual displacement/velocity, support correction, collider frame, camera position, air/landing/bail signals and emitted trick events.

| Test | Proposed acceptance evidence |

Invalid/zero/non-finite normals reject safely. |

Low-speed energy gives a useful independent sanity check: without pumping or ollie, 4 m/s under 22 m/s² gravity can gain only about 0.36 m height before losses. A low-speed trial reaching a 2.4 m lip would require an explicitly explained energy source. Conversely, a failed high-speed case may indicate collision/frame trouble rather than insufficient approach energy; retain its trace before tuning.
