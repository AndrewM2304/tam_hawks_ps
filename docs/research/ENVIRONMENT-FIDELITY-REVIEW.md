# Current-map environment fidelity and asset pipeline review

Owner request: review the last hardening turn, improve believable Glasgow art and rendering efficiency, and investigate Blender MCP without spending money or expanding the map. Inspected **17 October 2026**. This is a source/image audit and an implementation specification; proposed budgets below are targets to measure, not measured Apple Silicon limits. No new assets were installed in the game and no graphical/profile/test job was launched for this review.

## What the last pass improved, and what still reads as a prototype

The source reduces the grass shader's high-frequency grain, uses broader water normals, sets ACES tone mapping, and preserves a visible third-person rider. Those repairs are useful. They do not yet supply the material, silhouette, vegetation and contact-detail work needed for the owner's realistic-but-fun target.

Actual images inspected: [ordinary spawn](../testing/screenshots/playability-repair/02-normal-spawn.png), [park taper](../testing/screenshots/playability-repair/15-park-repaired-taper.png), and [Clyde crossing](../testing/screenshots/playability-repair/19-clyde-safe-bridge.png). The spawn court has readable architecture and real facade depth, but repeated stone/paving, square edges and thin repeated windows dominate. The park is a continuous dark green surface under sparse foliage, with untextured cylindrical trunks. The Clyde has the recognisable suspension shape and lower quays; the water still reads as a solid teal strip and the far bank as a repeated facade row. The text overlay is clearly present in these historical images; the owner now wants district-entry text removed. The separate UI implementation should follow that instruction and keep location discovery grounded in architecture and physical signage.

| Visible bottleneck | Exact implementation evidence | Best first change |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated flat materials over the largest screen areas | `city_art.gd::_surface_texture()` makes 256×256 albedo tiles for masonry, paving, slate and asphalt. The procedural tiles have generated mipmaps and the materials already use linear mipmap anisotropic filtering. They do not have authored roughness/normal maps. | Replace one foreground paving material and one sandstone trim material with physically scaled PBR detail, restrained variation and shared trim UVs. Keep mipmaps/filtering. |
| Sharp, repeated box silhouettes | `facade_kit.gd` builds useful 0.5 m shop depth, returns, sills, cornices and roof details, but most elements use `BoxMesh`; windows repeat in fixed columns/rows. | Author one bevelled facade bay and coping/ledge kit with shaped sills, weathered lower courses and varied shutters/paint. Reuse it with a small number of material variants. |
| Grass/tree/water material identity is weak | `outdoor_routes.gd` grass provides two broad colour-noise layers and constant roughness. `vegetation.gd` batches ten-sided untextured trunks. `foliage_mesh.gd` uses 660 opaque folded leaves and 44 branch pieces per crown (3,080 triangles), with vertex colour only. Water uses a broad analytic normal and conservative specular. | Add grass/soil/path contact bands and a bark/leaf material set, then tune Clyde water against its sky/bank context. Increasing the terrain triangle count alone would leave these flat colours. |

The imported ZC brick/concrete source images are already about 3K, while tracked `.import` files limit them to **1K**, enable mipmaps, and provide VRAM-compressed S3TC/BPTC and ETC2/ASTC variants. Only `warehouse_brick` and `cut_stone` receive these external albedo/normal maps via `_apply_external_materials()`. General sandstone, slate, path and most paving still use the procedural tiles. General and imported materials both use world-space triplanar mapping; this is useful for blockout but samples more than a simple UV-mapped material and cannot provide authored trim placement. A blanket “raise every texture to 4K” change would increase memory and bandwidth without solving geometry repetition, material scale or empty contact detail.

## Improve detail while rendering less

The game already renders the camera frustum, and `skater.gd` already sets the camera far plane to **220 m**. The sun shadows extend **75 m**. A lower far plane can remove distant draw work, but it does not unload assets, stop scripts/physics, or eliminate objects hidden behind other buildings. Broad `MultiMesh` bounds also keep hidden instances in a visible batch. In particular, `ClydeLandmarks._flush()` batches the bridge and three terraces together by material across a large area, and park trees share broad trunk/crown batches. Existing per-building facade batches are a better starting granularity.

1. **Measure the existing workload, then split broad art batches.** Use 25–40 m visual cells for the park/river, preserving shared mesh/material resources. The renderer can reject each cell's bounds. Avoid one node per leaf/window; retain `MultiMesh` per cell/material. Split the 900 m scenic water strip into a few coherent spans if its large bounds prove costly; avoid cracks and new material variants. Keep every current physical surface and rail registered independently of visual visibility.
2. **Introduce a detailed near mesh and a cheaper mid/far replacement.** Godot imported glTF scenes receive automatic mesh LOD; current runtime-created `ArrayMesh`/primitive batches do not acquire an import-time LOD chain. Use native visibility ranges for authored replacements and a 4–8 m hysteresis margin. Hard/hysteretic switches are the first Compatibility option; alpha fade temporarily adds transparent work. Do not hide a whole district based only on the skater's district ID: turns, high ollies and bridge views can see adjacent districts.
3. **Reduce material/submission count before raising triangles.** Bake the many small opaque facade pieces into modular meshes sharing a trim atlas. Keep transparent shop glass to a limited near range or use opaque window reflection/interior cards where suitable. Keep the Barrowland lettering and visible grind edges legible as detail reduces. Detail meshes must never become new skating colliders.
4. **Test static occluders where buildings actually block sight.** Use simple building-mass `BoxOccluder3D`/`QuadOccluder3D` shapes around existing solid architecture; leave archways, closes, windows and skating mouths open. Godot's automatic occluder baking ignores `MultiMeshInstance3D`, so these batches need deliberate occluder proxies. Occlusion has CPU cost and offers less on the open park/river. Keep it only if the same moving route improves on the target Mac.
5. **Choose a measured draw-distance profile.** Start with 220 m far plane and near-detail limits below. Trial a 160–180 m low-detail profile only with a low-detail landmark/skyline layer, matching fog and cameras at the park crest/quay. Avoid visible building/bridge disappearance. Do not make detail render distance control physics or kill combo/rail availability.

The historical six-view profile in [PERFORMANCE](../testing/PERFORMANCE.md) recorded approximately **1,892 median draw calls at Exchange** and **1,900 in the Barras near-hall view**, versus 211 at the park. It used an older source snapshot and CPU llvmpipe software rendering. Those figures identify useful places to inspect material submissions; they do not establish current Mac FPS or prove any proposed optimization. A moving camera route, shadow passes, transparency and frame-time spikes matter more than a stationary screenshot FPS claim.

## Trial asset budgets for the existing map

Distances are camera-to-asset trial ranges, with adjacent levels overlapping through hysteresis. Triangle counts are **per representative object/module**; total instance count and material submissions must also be measured. Preserve landmark shapes at distance rather than retaining tiny repeated ornaments.

| Current-map asset | Near, roughly 0–25 m | Mid, roughly 25–80 m | Far, roughly 80–220 m | Shared material/texture ceiling for first pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5–7 m facade bay | 8–16K triangles; bevelled reveals, sill/cornice, shallow shop scene | 2–4K; baked upper detail and opaque glass | 200–800 per bay, or 2–5K per joined building block | 2–3 opaque surfaces plus at most 1 near glass surface; shared 2K base/normal/ORM trim set |
| Existing fountain/coping/ledge module | 3–8K, shape detail focused on close edges and curved silhouette | 800–2K | 100–400; preserve grindable outline in view | 1–2 surfaces, shared 2K stone and 1K metal; original grind line/collision stays authoritative |
| Existing bridge portal/iron section | 5–15K portal or 1–3K repeated iron section | 2–5K portal, 200–600 section | 1–3K bridge silhouette with continuous cables | 2–3 surfaces using shared stone/paint/metal; no separate material for each fastener |
| Existing tree | 6–10K including shaped trunk and denser crown | 1.5–3K | 150–500 crown clusters, or validated impostor | Bark 1K and leaf atlas 1K–2K; at most 2 surfaces, cells not world-wide batches |
| Small market prop/stall dressing | 0.5–3K prop / 5–10K complete dressed stall | 100–500 prop / 1–2K stall | merge into market mass or omit tiny props | 1 shared 2K atlas for cloth/wood/wares plus existing common metal |
| Ground and grass edge | Retain terrain shape; 0.3–1K triangles per 4–8 m decorative edge patch only where visible | material transitions; no fine grass clumps | base terrain material | One 2K ground set plus a reusable mask; no dense blades over every hill |
| Skyline block | Detailed roofline only if approached | 1–3K block | 300–1K block; no individual mullions/books | 1–2 atlas surfaces, 1K shared atlas |

A useful initial **whole-view trial budget** is 400–700K visible main-pass triangles and 300–500 main-pass draw submissions at a busy court view, with quality judged at 1280×800 and 1920×1080. These are engineering allocation targets, **not promises of 60 fps** or the current measured totals. Count shadow submissions separately. If a near upgrade fits its per-asset triangle budget but adds dozens of surfaces it can still be a performance regression. If the target Mac reaches the frame-time goal with a higher triangle count and fewer submissions, retain the better result.

Use roughly 512–1024 texels per metre for an inspected foreground stone/paint trim and 128–256 texels per metre for ordinary mid-distance surfaces. Select tile scale from known paving/block sizes, not texture pixel count. A shared 2K set is usually enough for the representative bay; reserve a 4K texture for a hero surface only after close-camera comparison justifies it. Three uncompressed RGBA 2K images occupy 48 MiB before mipmaps (about 64 MiB with a full chain); file download/JPEG size is not GPU memory. Preserve runtime compression and report the actual renderer's texture memory before raising limits globally.

## Blender and Blender MCP

**Blender is useful here; Blender MCP is optional.** `/usr/bin/blender --version` reports **4.3.2**. No callable Blender MCP tool is exposed in this session. Existing headless Blender and a checked-in Python generator can already produce bevelled meshes, deterministic UVs, authored materials, baked detail, decimation/LOD variants, rigged glTF and repeatable exports. A manual artist could also edit the same source `.blend` later. Save source assets outside the runtime Godot import root, and commit exported `.glb`/external textures so the owner's Godot launch does not require Blender installed on the Mac.

The inspected third-party [MCP for Blender source](https://github.com/ahujasid/blender-mcp/tree/7a0373ec9199183cb460068c4f96aed9c579fb4f), MIT licence, is pinned at **`7a0373ec9199183cb460068c4f96aed9c579fb4f`** (repository commit 2026-10-06). Its README lists live Python execution, scene queries, multi-angle/wireframe/animation-strip inspection, material/object operations and Poly Haven search/import. Those are useful when interactively refining an asset in a running Blender UI. They do not automatically turn a vague prompt into a production-ready, licensed, low-draw-call game asset. UV scale, topology, PBR export, LOD, collision and in-game lighting still need inspection. Its optional generative integrations and premium package can involve paid services; they are outside this project's no-spend workflow. No MCP installation or API/generator call was made.

For this cloud checkout, a deterministic **Blender Python → glTF 2.0 → Godot** pipeline is the lowest-friction first step. Use MCP later if live modelling/visual feedback becomes a real bottleneck; installing it alone will not improve the existing art. The user's local Chrome/Blender applications are not implicitly reachable through the cloud filesystem.

Representative export contract:

- Metres; named objects; applied transforms and consistent pivots; collision/grind lines remain the existing Godot gameplay source.
- Original source `.blend` plus generator/config; actual geometry exported as `.glb`, or `.gltf` with shared external textures when clearer for reuse/version review. OBJ remains adequate for a static mesh but lacks skeleton/animation, UV2 and full PBR scene data.
- One shared Principled/PBR material set using base colour as sRGB and normal/ORM as non-colour data; roughness in ORM G and metallic in B, AO in R. Preserve OpenGL +Y normals. Check normal strength and tangent basis on the actual imported mesh.
- Bake procedural Blender-only nodes to exportable maps. Enable material backface culling for opaque architecture. Do not leave all meshes double-sided; test leaves separately. Preserve hard normals where edges require them, and inspect glTF's final vertex/triangle count because UV/normal splits can increase vertices.
- Enable/audit imported mesh LOD. Protect thin rail/landmark silhouettes from automatic decimation or supply authored replacements. Keep manual LOD surfaces and material slots aligned. All exports receive import, silhouette/wireframe, moving chase-view and renderer checks.
