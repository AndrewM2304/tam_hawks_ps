## Composition and implementation boundaries

Use three restrained depth bands. **Near:** real terrain/building/retaining footing matching current support and containment; fill only actual exposed dry edges. **Middle:** retained sandstone/brick roofs, slate/chimneys, station or market backs and river frontage give local scale/parallax. **Far:** a static original sky panorama can supply muted hills/woodland, a varied low/mid-rise city and a few taller accents without another mesh city. A distant panorama is a candidate, not an already tested solution.

For a Glasgow-inspired silhouette, favour a broad sandstone/slate/chimney roof rhythm with sparse tower/spire/crane or taller modern-block accents and subdued hill profiles, rather than uniform skyscrapers around every compass point. The owner wants hills/buildings/tall background forms; use those to create bearings without burying the gallery clock, Barrowland sign or bridge portals. Existing reference research supplies local architectural cues; this review has not verified a surveyed 360° Glasgow panorama and must not claim exact geographic sightlines. Create original scenery; no bundled Maps/Street View photographs or unlicensed panoramic photo.

Keep the far layer quiet: low-frequency silhouettes, atmospheric separation, no tiny repeated emissive windows, animated noise or per-frame sky regeneration. Retain current gameplay, lighting intent and skyline distance; if sky changes ambient/reflections, expose and measure that separately rather than inadvertently retuning all materials. Do not shorten the far plane, hide the surroundings with thicker fog or move the camera closer to conceal gaps. No new collision, route, checkpoint, goal, encounter or playable area belongs in the panorama.

Near geometry remains necessary where camera movement exposes an edge below the horizon, especially park high points. Reuse existing boundary bodies/faces/materials where possible. A painted distant ground band must never be treated as route support, collision repair or permission to remove real containment. A panorama's zero translational parallax is acceptable for distant scenery; it looks false if a near tenement with readable large windows appears at the end of a short alley.
