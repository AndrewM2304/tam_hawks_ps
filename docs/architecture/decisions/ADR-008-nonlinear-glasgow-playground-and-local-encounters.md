# ADR-008: A nonlinear Glasgow playground with original local encounters

**Status:** Accepted owner direction; reference audit and implementation in progress. **Date:** 17 October 2026. **Owners:** A01/A02 integration/design, A03 reference/licence research, A05 level/characters, A06/A07 encounters and A09 independent verification.

## Context and authority

The owner asked for a stronger level-design comparison with San Francisco, London and Los Angeles in the Tony Hawk series, and the School levels in its first two games. The requested outcome is a distinctive, nonrectangular skating playground with recognisable areas, corridors, obstacles and multiple choices of line. The earlier six-area redesign adds height and named places, but those changes alone do not establish interesting route choices or an enjoyable level.

The owner also requested Glasgow character and humour, citing local dancers, buskers and cheeky interactions. These are creative references to investigate, not permission to claim that real people endorse or appear in this project. This authorized work extends ADR-007 and the unfinished capture, verification, export and handoff work; it does not replace those obligations or restart the project.

## Decision

1. Study the named reference levels for transferable design principles: connected loops, visible choices, different route heights, short technical cuts beside forgiving returns, landmarks that aid orientation, and obstacles that let the player invent lines. Record what was actually inspected, source/date and uncertainty. A remembered feature or audit recommendation is not an implemented feature. Author original geometry; do not trace proprietary maps or reproduce distinctive sequences, assets, names, music or voices.
2. Evaluate this level as a route graph and as a playable place. Add the strongest feasible nonlinear connections to the existing six areas: useful cross-links, readable forks and alternate routes that reconnect rather than merely extending a corridor. Preserve broad spaces for recovery and the established career circuit. Each new connection must have a stated skating purpose, supported surface, visible arrival and a return or clearly labelled jump risk.
3. Keep routes and the production map driven by runtime geometry data. New physical connectors require corresponding ordered route definitions, ground queries, labels where useful and independent traversal tests. A line drawn on the map is not proof of a traversable route. Avoid silently presenting optional jumps as safe ground connections.
4. Add original fictional Glasgow locals and optional encounters using inspected cultural references, original dialogue and compatible free assets. The first implementation target is a busker challenge: accept an offer, use a varied line and bank at least 1,000 points through ordinary scoring. Exact eligible trick families, locality, reset and repeat rules are specified before implementation and verified against actual input. Additional dancer or harmless slapstick ideas remain proposals until separately implemented and tested.
5. Use local humour through place, timing, character and playful consequences. Do not substitute a derogatory label for a character, copy a real person's likeness/catchphrases/voice or imply their participation. Any slapstick mechanic needs a recoverable fictional interaction and clear, bounded gameplay behavior. No real recordings, licensed songs or paid voice service are introduced without compatible permission and the existing zero-spend constraint.
6. Preserve the original ten career goal IDs, four required career districts, earned records/unlocks, save schema and free-skate restrictions. Optional local challenges are distinct from the canonical career checklist. A cosmetic/side reward must use its explicitly agreed contract; do not silently alter total career completion, unlock gates or permanent save data.

## Evidence and remaining work

The lead audit supplies prioritized recommendations and observed reference patterns; the implementation owner records which were actually built. Acceptance combines a useful route graph with real player motion: independent paths reach their destinations, cross-links rejoin, seams do not snag, risky lines have readable alternatives and missed attempts recover safely. The existing ten-goal career route and all scoring/input/persistence checks must still pass.

Capture the final world after geometry, encounters and HUD settle. Keep normal chase views, overview, actual ready map and actual encounter/challenge states, with exact source hashes and fixture limitations. A positioned screenshot cannot certify a completed challenge; a deterministic route cannot certify discovery, turning comfort or fun. Preserve the earlier 871-check result as a checkpoint preceding the later visual/layout/encounter changes, not a pass for untested source.
